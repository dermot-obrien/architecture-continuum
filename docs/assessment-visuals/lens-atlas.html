The assessment lenses

A lens is a set of criteria applied to a design specification. The core lens applies to every design. The others stack on top when the design contains a model or an agent, or when the solution is going into production. 121 criteria in four lenses, each one saying what it reads, who decides it and how much a failure matters.

Anatomy

What one criterion says

1id: CORE-SEC-02
2area: SEC
  question: Is every connector that crosses a
    trust boundary authenticated and authorised?
3reads: [connectors.authentication,
    connectors.authorisation, elements.trust_zone]
4decided_by: tool
5rule: Every connector whose ends lie in
    different trust zones has authentication and
    authorisation other than none.
6severity: blocker
7sources: [zt-800-207, wa-aws-sec]
  1. A permanent id. Prefix, area, number. An id is never reused, so a finding in one assessment can be compared with the next.
  2. One question a design can answer. Grouped into areas, each with a question of its own.
  3. The fields it reads. Dotted paths into the design specification, so the lens doubles as the author's list of what to write.
  4. Who decides. A tool, a reviewer, or both: the tool checks the answer is there, the reviewer judges whether it is good enough.
  5. A rule a tool can apply without judgement. Required for tool criteria. This is what makes the check depth run on every change.
  6. A default severity. An assessor may change it with a reason. One open blocker means the design is not endorsed.
  7. Its sources, by key, resolved in the lens's source list.
Boundaries

What is not a lens, and what could be next

Separate assessments, not lenses

  • Cybersecurity: are controls implemented and effective?
  • Privacy impact
  • AI impact
  • Regulatory conformity

Each one reads the running system, follows its own method, often needs an accredited assessor, and ends in an acceptance only its owner can give. They meet the lenses as a hand-over at design review and as evidence at production entry.

Candidate lenses

  • Privacy by design, brought in by a personal-data profile
  • Data platforms
  • Regulated industries
  • Sovereign hosting
  • Readiness, after TOGAF's Business Transformation Readiness Assessment

A new lens needs a profile or a trigger and nothing else; the standard doesn't change. A changed criterion moves the lens's minor version. A change that would turn earlier results wrong moves the major version.
