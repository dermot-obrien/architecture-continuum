One assessment core for architecture and cybersecurity

Both practices compare evidence about a subject against criteria, judge each criterion, find risks and decide what to do about them. What differs is the subject, where the criteria come from, the methods, who may assess, and what the verdict permits. The shared part is the core below; the differences become two profiles.

The shared core

The axis that separates them: assurance depth

A requirement can be assessed at four depths, and each depth needs different evidence. Architecture assessment works at the first two; cybersecurity assessment at the last two. The same requirement, such as "a T1 limit is enforced outside the agent", gets one criterion at each depth, so a single model holds both assessments without either taking over the other's question.

What generalises

  • The assessment, its subject version, its criteria sets and its assessors.
  • Criterion, observation, finding and risk, with their links.
  • Responses and the owner who accepts residual risk.
  • Reliance: one assessment citing another's results.
  • The result scale, taking the architecture scale as the superset.

What stays specific

  • How the subject is described: a design specification, or a security plan.
  • Where criteria come from, and their content.
  • Which methods and evidence count.
  • Who may assess, and whether accreditation is required.
  • What a verdict permits: building a design, or operating a system.

How to build it

  1. Split the assessment schema into a core and an architecture profile.
  2. Add an assurance depth and tests to every criterion.
  3. Add observations, reliance and OSCAL's risk status values to the core.
  4. Map the core to OSCAL for the security profile, rather than writing a second security schema.
