OSCAL: from a control catalogue to a finding and a plan of action

Seven of OSCAL's eight models in their three layers, the assemblies inside them that an assessment touches, and the fields that link them. Every name on the page is OSCAL's own. The eighth model, mapping-collection, maps controls between catalogues and is left out.

Reading it left to right

  • A profile selects and tailors controls from a catalog: a baseline.
  • A system-security-plan says how a system inside its authorization-boundary implements each control, component by component.
  • An assessment-plan says which controls and subjects will be assessed; assessment-results record what was observed, what was found and which risks remain.
  • The plan-of-action-and-milestones carries open risks forward with their remediation.

The links that matter for assessment

  • target: a finding is about one control objective or statement, with status.state of satisfied or not-satisfied.
  • related-observations: the evidence a finding or risk rests on, kept apart from the judgement.
  • related-risks: what a deficiency exposes, with its own status and remediations.
  • mitigating-factors: the only control-to-risk link. It runs from the risk to an implementation in the security plan (implementation-uuid), not from a catalogue control, and it is optional.
  • threat-ids: a risk names threats by an external identifier and source (system, href). OSCAL does not model threats, and a catalogue control names no risk or threat at all.

Checked against OSCAL 1.2.3

  • Every assembly and link on the page exists in the 1.2.3 Metaschema sources and the generated JSON Schema.
  • Catalogue parts are named assessment-objective and assessment-method; objective has been deprecated since 1.0.1. A finding's target still uses the type objective-id.
  • Values: finding state satisfied, not-satisfied; observation methods EXAMINE, INTERVIEW, TEST, UNKNOWN; risk status open, investigating, remediating, deviation-requested, deviation-approved, closed.
