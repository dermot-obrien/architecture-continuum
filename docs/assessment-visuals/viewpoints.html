Viewpoint 2 · Design author

Getting a design ready to be assessed

The assessment reads what you wrote, and only that. A quality the design meets but does not state counts as a quality it does not meet.

  1. Read the lensesEach criterion names the fields it reads, so the lens is also your list of what to write.
  2. Write the specificationThe design as YAML against the schema. Cite catalogue ids, never copy them.
  3. Run the check yourselfValidate, then walk the 53 tool criteria by their rules.
  4. Clear the blockersOne open blocker ends in not endorsed, and it cannot be accepted as a risk.
  5. Explain, don't defendPoint to where the specification says it. Say plainly where it doesn't.
  6. Act on dispositionsFix, raise a consideration, record a decision, take on debt, or have the owner accept the risk.

What the specification holds, and the blockers it answers

driversGoals, non-goals, stakeholders with concerns · CORE-DEM-01
contextCapabilities realised, platform, contexts, use cases
elementsEvery box typed by continuum kind, owned, placed
connectorsContract, auth across trust zones, untrusted content · CORE-SEC-02
scenariosQuality with a measurable response · CORE-QA-01
fitness_functionsMeasure, threshold, when it runs
decisionsAlternatives, rationale, reversibility, departures from the target state
considerationsEvery open question, with options
risksKnown risks with owner and mitigation
operationsSLOs, observability, recovery, cost, support
evidenceThreat model, cost model, tests, for this version
all referencesEvery id resolves · CORE-STR-05

Gaps reviewers find most often

  • "Fast" or "highly available" with no number attached
  • No failure scenario for a dependency the team does not control
  • A connector across a trust boundary with authentication set to none
  • A decision with no alternative, or a one-way door not marked as one
  • Open questions left in prose, where no tool can see them
  • Evidence produced for an earlier version of the design
Write down the risks you know about.A risk you record with an owner is a finding the assessor doesn't have to make. A risk you had seen and left out reads worse than one nobody saw.
Viewpoint 3 · Assessor

Running an assessment

Follow the eight steps of ISO/IEC/IEEE 42020's evaluation process, leave every decidable criterion to the tool, and spend reviewer time on judgement.

  1. PrepareFix the design id, version and digest, the depth and the assessors.
  2. Select lensesDerive profiles from the facets and compare with the declared ones.
  3. CheckValidate, then run the tool criteria. If it doesn't validate, stop.
  4. ReviewAnswer every remaining criterion. "Not assessed" must say why.
  5. EvaluateAt evaluation depth: utility tree, sensitivity and trade-off points.
  6. DisposeGive every shortfall a severity and a home on the continuum.
  7. DecideReach the outcome and the conformance level by the rules.
  8. Record and hand overWrite the record beside the design and open the hand-overs.

Who decides each criterion, by lens (120 in all)

Tool decides (53)Tool checks presence, reviewer judges (39)Reviewer decides (29)

How the outcome is reached, first rule that applies

OutcomeRule
deferredCan't be carried out: the specification doesn't validate, or derives a higher tier than its triage. Missing evidence is a finding, not a deferral
not endorsedAny open blocker, or an open major that is a design fix with no due date
with conditionsOpen majors remain, each disposed of otherwise or carrying a due date
endorsedNo open blocker or major

Finding types come from ATAM: gap, risk, non-risk, sensitivity point, trade-off point, observation. At least one assessor must not have written or delivered the design. An agent may assess, with provenance; from AT2 an independent person reviews its findings, and a person reaches the outcome.

Viewpoint 4 · Service owner

Approving a solution for production

The production entry assessment checks that what was built is the design that was assessed, and that it can be run. You approve or decline it, by name and date.

The evidence on your desk, all for the version being released

Prior design reviewEndorsed, conditions closed · PRD-BLD-01
As-built recordWhat was built, every departure · PRD-BLD-02
Hand-over outcomesSecurity, privacy, AI impact, conformity; none failed · PRD-HND-01
Rollback testDone, with the point of no return known · PRD-REL-01
Recovery testAgainst the stated RTO and RPO
Load and acceptancePeak load; every functional scenario end to end
Support teamHours, on-call, escalation · PRD-OPS-01
Readiness reviewRunbook, alerting on every SLO
Residual riskOwned by someone entitled to accept it · PRD-RSK-01

As built against as designed

What you are acceptingEvery risk, debt item and accepted finding carried into production. Conditions need an owner and a due date, never sit on a blocker, and the record schedules the first periodic assessment. A solution that departs from its design needs a new, assessed version of the design. A condition won't cover it.
Viewpoint 5 · Security, privacy and compliance

Where architecture ends and your assessment begins

The two overlap on threat modelling and nowhere else. Your assessments are not lenses: they meet the architecture assessment twice, as a hand-over and as evidence.

Why your assessment is not a lens

A lensYour assessment
ReadsThe design specificationThe running system, configuration, tests, logs
MethodThis standard'sIts own: NIST SP 800-53A, ISO/IEC 27001 audit, ISO/IEC 42005
AssessorArchitects, no accreditationOften accredited or legally accountable
Ends inFindings and an outcomeAn acceptance, authorisation or certificate only its owner can give

What you receive, and what you return

  • At design review: a hand-over naming the scope and the findings that prompted it, plus the threat model for that version
  • Obligations already named, with how the design classes under each
  • Before production: your outcome for the version being released, recorded as evidence. A failed outcome is a blocker
  • The residual risks you report, which the production entry assessment checks are accepted by the person you name

The design-facing part of privacy can be a lens. A privacy-by-design lens, brought in by a personal-data profile, is a candidate.

Viewpoint 6 · AI and agent teams

What changes when a design uses models or agents

A model is a technology or SBB with an ai facet; an agent is a service that also has an agent facet. The facets bring in the extra lenses, and the core lens still applies.

AI solution lens · 28 criteria, 3 blockers

  • Why a model at all, and what the deterministic alternative would cost
  • Model pinned, carded, replaceable, with a fallback
  • Evals with a metric and threshold, gating release, calibrated judges AIS-EVAL-01
  • Retrieval trimmed to what the caller may see AIS-DATA-02
  • Output constrained and validated before it reaches a tool, store, query, code or user AIS-IO-02
  • False positives and false negatives capped separately by cost, not F1 alone; abstention tested; zero-tolerance leakage eval
  • Token, rate, cost and time limits; tracing; human oversight; disclosure; regulatory class

At production entry

  • Gating evals pass on the release candidate
  • Drift monitoring and an online eval switched on
  • For an agent, a control test shows its limits and kill switch work without its cooperation

Agent lens · 23 criteria, 9 blockers. The one that decides most designs:

AGT-INJ-01 No session holds all three. If one must, it doesn't run unattended. The other blockers: every action listed with its reversibility; dangerous actions approved or behind a T0 or T1 limit; limits held outside the agent; sandboxed code; its own identity; authenticated agent-to-agent traffic; step, cost and time limits with a kill switch; autonomy proportionate to impact.

Source: the draft Architecture Assessment Standard 0.5.0 and its core, ai-solution, ai-agent and production lenses, in the architecture continuum bundle, 28 October 2026. Counts are read from the lens files. The standard is a draft. Its tool criteria are specified but not yet implemented.
